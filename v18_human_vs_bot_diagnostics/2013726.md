## Items

### T3 — blue defers its reaction to give a 3 save

- **Human:** blue need not defer, because it knows it is playing exactly the r1.
  Green sees that its Bob has a critical card on chop, and it has a simple stable
  clue to get the r2 played if needed. The preemptive 3 save is still acceptable.
- **Bot:** blue gave 3 to black (`reactor0.choose_very_high_clue`, rung
  `1.reactive_play`, value 3). Stacks at T3 are `0010` in every view.
- **Status:** not to be fixed (ruling).

### T5 — green fixes blue's dead called card with a 2

- **Human:** no clue is needed.
  - Blue's chop is trash (its o14).
  - Green has its own queued play.
  - Green's called card `{r2,b2}` could be the dupe.
  - If a fix were needed, Blue is strictly better than 2: blue could then pass its
    b2 back if green's called card is the b2.
- **Bot:**
  - **Rung.** Green gave 2 to blue through rung `2b.fix` (shape `trash_reveal`,
    `reactor0.choose_very_high_clue`).
  - **Blue's hand.** Blue's o14 (slot 1, the chop) was called to play as `{r1,b2}`
    and is really the r1, already down (true stacks `1010`). The 2 touched blue's
    o11, the b2.
  - **The fix.** Its negative touch cut o14 to `{r1}`, which is the fix, but it also
    left o11 reading `{r2,b2}`.
  - **Green's hand.** Green's called o7 `{r2,b2}` is the r2.
  - **Stacks.** `1010` in the private views and in pair(green, blue); `0010` common.
- **Cause:** the very-high fix rung fires whenever a standing call is dead. It
  doesn't ask whether the holder will throw the card anyway (it is the holder's
  chop, or a dupe discharge), or whether the giver has a play of its own. And it
  doesn't prefer a colour fix, which lets the holder pass back, over a rank fix.
- **Status:** open (planned v18.6.0).

### T17 — green does not save blue's playable g1 on chop

- **Human:** blue's g1 on chop is in danger. Black has trash on chop, so blue has
  no clue to give and will discard. A human clues 1 or Green to blue.
- **Bot:**
  - Green played its known b3 (rung `8.lowest_stack_rank`).
  - `reactor0.choose_clue` saw one candidate, and no rung matched.
  - At T18 blue discarded o24, the g1.
- **Stacks at T17:** green private `2021`; blue and black `3021`; true `3021`.
- **Cause:** no clue rung saves a *playable* card that isn't critical and is about
  to be discarded because its holder has nothing better to do.
- **Status:** open (planned v18.7.0).

### T27 — 4 to green is an illegal pairing, so blue gives Red to black

- **Human:** in the endgame (pace ≤ 1 with 3 players), the bucket rule should not
  bind. 4 to green is reactive: it gets black's r4 and green's `{g1,n3}`.
- **Bot:** blue gave Red to black (rung `3.bob_chop`, shape `stable_play`), calling
  black's o30 as the r4. Stacks: blue and black `3052`, green `2052`, true `3052`.
- **Cause:** a reactive whose pairing breaks the bucket relation is legal to give
  only when both players can already name their cards (`both_know_their_own`,
  `src/conventions/tiiah/interpret_reactive.cpp:147-155`). There is no endgame
  exception.
- **Status:** open (planned v18.4.0).

### T30 — no reverse reactive: blue gives a stable 3 to black

- **Human:** reverse reactives are useful in difficult TIIAH variants and must be
  available. Brown to black is a reverse-reactive finesse of n3 into n4.
- **Bot:**
  - Blue gave 3 to black (rung `4.locked_eight_clues_or_low_pace`, shape
    `stable_discard`), and green did not react.
  - Black held o30, called to play as the r4 by the T27 Red, with touches r1–r5.
  - Stacks: private `3152` at every seat, but pair(blue, green) is `2152` at blue
    and `3152` at green (`≠`), and common is `2152` at blue and black versus `3152`
    at green.
- **Cause:** since v18.0.0 the reverse-reactive position requires Bob to hold a
  *touch-known* play (`has_known_play`, `src/conventions/variants/hole.cpp:61-80`).
  A called card whose touches allow unplayable identities doesn't count, so the
  position never held and the reverse arm was unavailable.
  - The view disagreement at T30 is a separate bookkeeping issue, recorded here for
    follow-up.
- **Resolution (v18.3.0):**
  - **Rule:** the reverse-reactive position now keys on a *standing play*: a
    touch-known play, or a **clued** card called to play (`has_standing_play`,
    `src/conventions/variants/hole.cpp:82-97`). Role inversion is the clue-to-Cathy
    half of the same position.
  - **Rerun:** `build/replay_log.exe logs/yagami_blue-2013726.log --turn 30 --rerun`
    gave the stable 3 (`clue rank value=3 → player 0`) on v18.2.0, and gives Brown
    to black (`clue colour value=3 → player 0`, `1.reactive_play`) on v18.3.0.
  - **Test:**
    `tests/test_tiiah/test_replay_2013726_reverse_reactive_finesse_on_a_called_play.cpp`.
  - **Self-play**, seeds 1–300 at 6 s, v18.2.0 → v18.3.0:

    | | 25/25 | Cards read wrongly (per 100 games) | Strikeouts |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | v18.2.0 | 35 | 383 | 74 |
    | v18.3.0 (clued calls only) | 34 | 422 | 88 |
    | Tried first: every call, clued or not | 27 | 436 | 95 |

    The first try is why unclued calls are left out. The change shipped by ruling.
    Which reverse reactives go wrong in self-play is still open.
- **Status:** fixed in v18.3.0.

### T38 (sample line) — no reactive target once every target is gotten

- **Human:** the reactive walk skips cards already gotten (called). Once every
  playable or finesse target in the receiver's hand is gotten, the walk should run
  as if none were, and take the leftmost target that works, as outside TIIAH. Then
  Brown to black can get both n5 and g4.
- **Cause:** the TIIAH target walk skips every CALLED_TO_PLAY card and has no
  fallback (`src/conventions/tiiah/interpret_reactive.cpp:215-217`).
- **Status:** open (planned v18.5.0). There is no log, so it needs a fixture test.
