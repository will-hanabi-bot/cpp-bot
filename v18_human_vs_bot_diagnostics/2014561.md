### T49 — green gives 4 to blue, not Green on the g4

- **Bot:** green chose 4 on blue's o46, the g4, read as a stable discard
  (`3.bob_chop`, `stable_discard`).
- **Ruling:** no change. Green genuinely cannot know it played the g3: its hole
  card o3 was `{y3,g3,p3}` and its private stacks had green on 2 (`332523`). So
  from its seat the 4 is correct.
- **Guidance kept:** the general rule stated here is "tiebreak stable play clues
  by the number of inferences the receiver will make on their card". It is
  criterion 1 of the T50 hierarchy.
- **Status:** no change (ruling); the rule is carried by T50.

### T50 — blue gives 3 over Purple among three stable plays

- **Human:** a four-step hierarchy for choosing between stable play clues (above).
  Here it picks Purple, on criterion 3.
- **Bot:**
  - Blue gave 3 to black on o30, the p3 (rung 3.1, `stable_play`).
  - The other stable plays were Yellow (o47, the y4) and Purple (o30 p3, plus the
    clued o13 p4 and the o10 p1).
- **Cause:**
  - `stable_play_chain` (`src/conventions/reactor0/decision.cpp`) had one TIIAH term,
    "the receiver can name the card", and all three pass it.
  - The default score then decides: 1.99 × new useful touches − new trash touches.
    That ties 3 with Yellow and puts Purple below both, because its p1 counts as
    trash even though it is known trash.
- **Resolution (v18.17.0):** the **stable play hierarchy** (tiiah `CONVENTION.md`
  §2a, `settle_stable_play`). It is the tiebreak of every stable-play rung.
  - Its keys are read from the giver's model of the receiver after the clue.
  - "Unknown trash" is judged on the stacks the called play leaves, so Purple's
    o10 (`{p1,p3}`, the p3 being the called card) costs nothing.
  - Keys at T50: all three have 1 inference and no ancillary value. The product
    over black's other good cards is 176 for Purple, 540 for Yellow and 680 for 3.
  - Rerun, `logs/yagami_blue-2014561.log --turn 50`: `clue rank value=3 → player 2`
    before, `clue colour value=4 → player 2` (Purple) after. Green's T49 rerun is
    unchanged (4).
  - Tests:
    - `tests/test_tiiah/test_decision_making/test_replay_2014561_stable_play_hierarchy_prefers_purple.cpp`;
    - `tests/test_tiiah/test_decision_making/test_stable_play_hierarchy.cpp`, one
      tie per criterion.
- **Status:** fixed in v18.17.0.

### T56 — blue reveals a trash p1 instead of Yellow or Purple

- **Human:** Yellow or Purple (the y4 or the p4) is much better. If blue already
  played the other copy with its unknown 4, black will toss it.
- **Bot:**
  - Blue gave 1 to black on o10, the p1, as a trash reveal (rung 4).
  - Only 3 candidates survived analysis.
  - Blue's o46, an unknown 4, had gone into the hole at T53.
- **Cause:** §2c's own-dupe veto (`calls_a_card_we_may_have_played`) drops any clue
  calling a card that some world of our hole cards has already played.
- **Ruling:** lift the veto only for a stable play whose called card the receiver
  can name exactly. The receiver watched our hole card go in, so it can tell a dupe
  and throw it.
- **Resolution (v18.18.0):**
  - **Giver:** `calls_a_card_we_may_have_played` spares a stable play whose
    holder can name the called card exactly after the clue (tiiah `CONVENTION.md`
    §2c).
  - **Receiver:** `repin_own_call` (§1.3) handles a call the giver's frame names
    as an identity X, where the holder watched the giver throw an X into the hole
    unnamed. It reads the call as the dupe: known trash, call withdrawn, thrown.
    That is what makes the giver's call safe, and it was the user's ruling to add
    it.
  - Rerun, `logs/yagami_blue-2014561.log --turn 56`: `clue rank value=1 → player 2`
    (trash reveal) before, `clue colour value=1 → player 2` (Yellow) after.
  - Tests:
    - `tests/test_tiiah/test_decision_making/test_replay_2014561_named_stable_play_past_own_dupe_veto.cpp`;
    - `tests/test_tiiah/test_named_call_on_a_watched_dupe.cpp`.
  - **Engine fix found on the way:** self-play seed 100 surfaced a pre-existing
    bug. `on_clue` wrote a one-identity shared empathy over a card we can see, so
    a wrong count (Cathy's b2 left as `{b1}`) rewrote the deck, and a rank 1 aimed
    at the "b1" touched nothing. It now writes only a card we cannot see
    (`src/basics/game.cpp`).
- **Status:** fixed in v18.18.0.
